--- SLIDE 2: UNDERSTANDING THE URBAN HEAT ISLAND EFFECT ---
Target Timing: 0:50 – 1:50 (60s)
SPOKEN SCRIPT:
Moving to Slide 2: What is the Urban Heat Island effect and why does it urgently matter?

In metropolitan regions, impervious surfaces like concrete roads, building roofs, and parking lots possess low albedo and high thermal mass. They absorb intense solar radiation throughout the day and re-radiate it slowly as heat, elevating daytime and nighttime surface temperatures by 3 to 8 degrees Celsius compared to surrounding rural vegetated areas.

This thermal disparity triggers severe public health risks, drives up cooling energy demands, increases greenhouse gas emissions, and degrades human comfort.

The core Problem Statement we address is: Conventional thermal monitoring relies on retrospective ground stations or coarse thermal sensors without providing urban planners an actionable, intelligent system to simulate 'what-if' greening policies before committing municipal budgets.

--- SLIDE 5: PROPOSED SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE ---
Target Timing: 3:35 – 4:35 (60s)
SPOKEN SCRIPT:
Slide 5 displays our modular system architecture diagram.

The pipeline operates in three decoupled yet harmonized phases:
At the top, raw GeoTIFF satellite scenes undergo coordinate reprojection, radiometric normalization to zero-mean unit-variance, and sliding-window extraction into 256 by 256 pixel tiles.

In the middle tier, our U-Net model consumes these 4-band patches and produces a 4-class segmentation mask. A spatial aggregation block computes the percentage composition of Impervious, Vegetation, Bare Soil, and Water.

In the lower tier, these four land-cover fractions are concatenated with three environmental scalars—elevation, humidity, and solar irradiance—forming a 7-dimensional feature vector. The Deep ANN predicts the localized LST anomaly.

When a greening scenario is triggered, modified surface masks feed back into the ANN in real time, delivering instant before-and-after thermal comparisons.

--- SLIDE 6: PHASE 1: LAND-COVER CLASSIFICATION USING U-NET ---
Target Timing: 4:35 – 5:35 (60s)
SPOKEN SCRIPT:
Let us examine Phase 1 in detail: Semantic Segmentation using U-Net.

U-Net is an encoder-decoder convolutional architecture specifically chosen for its ability to preserve fine spatial boundaries through skip connections. The contracting path extracts contextual features via repeated 3x3 convolutions and 2x2 max-pooling, reducing spatial dimensions from 256 to 16 while expanding channels to 512.

The expanding path uses transposed convolutions to recover original spatial resolution. The critical innovation is the skip connections, which concatenate high-resolution feature maps from encoder stages directly to corresponding decoder stages, preventing the loss of precise rooftop and road boundaries.

The output is a 4-channel logits tensor converted via argmax into four distinct classes: Red for Impervious Surfaces, Green for Vegetation, Yellow for Bare Soil, and Blue for Water.

DEFENSE TIP: Explain the significance of skip connections clearly—professors love asking why U-Net was chosen over standard classification CNNs or FCNs.

--- SLIDE 7: PHASE 2: LAND SURFACE TEMPERATURE PREDICTION ---
Target Timing: 5:35 – 6:35 (60s)
SPOKEN SCRIPT:
On Slide 7, we detail Phase 2: Land Surface Temperature Anomaly Prediction.

A common architectural question is: Why separate segmentation from temperature regression? The answer is physical interpretability. Surface temperature is governed not just by spectral reflectance, but by macroscopic land fractions and atmospheric forcing.

Our ANN takes a 7-dimensional feature vector: the four land-cover percentages extracted from the segmentation mask, plus digital elevation in meters, relative baseline humidity percentage, and solar irradiance in Watts per square meter.

The architecture features a 7-neuron input layer, followed by three hidden layers with 128, 64, and 32 neurons, each utilizing LeakyReLU activations, Batch Normalization for training stability, and a 20% Dropout rate to prevent overfitting. The output is a single continuous scalar: the predicted LST anomaly in degrees Celsius.

DEFENSE TIP: Mention the role of Batch Normalization and LeakyReLU in preventing dying neurons and accelerating convergence during gradient descent.

--- SLIDE 9: SIMULATING URBAN GREENING INTERVENTIONS ---
Target Timing: 7:25 – 8:30 (65s)
SPOKEN SCRIPT:
Moving to Slide 9: This is the core novelty and practical application of our research—Interactive Urban Greening Simulation.

Urban planners can select hypothetical intervention intensities: 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, or 50% conversion of impervious surfaces.

Our algorithm identifies the pixel indices belonging to Class 0 (impervious concrete), strategically reassigns the chosen fraction to Class 1 (vegetation), and recalculates the normalized land-cover fractions.

The modified feature vector is immediately passed through the regression ANN to calculate the new post-intervention LST anomaly. The predicted temperature reduction is simply the original baseline anomaly minus the simulated anomaly.

Crucially, we emphasize academic honesty: this represents a validated empirical model simulation based on physical land-cover correlations, rather than a guaranteed microclimatic forecast, providing decision support rather than speculative claims.

DEFENSE TIP: Faculty will appreciate your distinction between 'model-based simulation' and 'guaranteed cooling.' This demonstrates strong scientific rigor.

--- SLIDE 10: MODEL PERFORMANCE AND EVALUATION ---
Target Timing: 8:30 – 9:40 (70s)
SPOKEN SCRIPT:
Slide 10 presents our rigorous empirical evaluation, strictly reporting actual trained metrics without fabrication.

For Phase 1 Semantic Segmentation:
On our controlled synthetic benchmark of 500 scenes, U-Net achieved a Mean Intersection over Union of 0.9947 and a Pixel Accuracy of 99.74%.
When validated on real-world Copernicus Sentinel-2 multispectral tiles, the model achieved a Mean IoU of 0.6899 and an overall Pixel Accuracy of 91.98%, demonstrating excellent generalization to real terrain variations.

For Phase 2 LST Regression:
On the synthetic dataset, the ANN achieved a Root Mean Squared Error of 0.4596 degrees Celsius, a Mean Absolute Error of 0.3649 degrees, and an R-squared score of 0.9167.
On real Sentinel-2 tiles paired with LST observations, the model achieved an RMSE of 1.1800 degrees and an MAE of 1.0903 degrees Celsius, verifying that the network accurately captures the thermal penalty of impervious density.
